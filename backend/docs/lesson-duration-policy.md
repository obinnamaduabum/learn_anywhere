# Lesson length and reading estimates

The authoring target is a complete, coherent learning objective with enough explanation and worked examples to be useful. Aim mainly for 5–10 minutes of reading; allow up to 15 when warranted. Avoid repetitive padding, artificial chapter breaks and required clicks after tiny passages. Keep practice and quiz time separate from reading time.

There is no universally established optimum lesson duration. A systematic review reports varied microlearning durations and no agreed ideal: [Microlearning beyond boundaries](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11774797/). This supports treating the target as a product choice that needs learner testing, rather than a guaranteed optimum.

[Brysbaert's reading-rate meta-analysis](https://biblio.ugent.be/publication/8647789) reports average adult English nonfiction silent reading of 238 words/minute. Its estimates are not universal across languages, abilities or reading tasks. This app uses 200 whitespace-delimited body words/minute as an explicit conservative planning assumption; it is not a measured rate for every learner.

For new course drafts:

- Count the lesson body, including the written worked examples and activity instructions; exclude quiz questions and time spent doing the activity.
- Set `minutes = ceil(wordCount / 200)`.
- Require 1,000–3,000 body words and a matching estimate of 5–15 minutes.
- Never clamp a short lesson to five minutes or a long one to fifteen. Expand substantive explanation, combine related sections, or split an overlong chapter.
- Audit translated bodies independently. Translation changes word counts; identical lesson identities do not imply identical duration labels.
- Human review checks accuracy, readability, pedagogy and language quality. Structural validation alone does not establish these.

Published packs remain immutable. Revised content and translations use a new version and remain drafts until explicitly approved. Existing downloaded versions and their progress are retained.
